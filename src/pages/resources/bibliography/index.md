### Genesis and Focus

The bibliography originated as a 1983 booklet expanding on nine readings Robert Brabenec assembled in 1975. A 1977 conference at Wheaton College initiated the biennial conference series for the Association of Christians in the Mathematical Sciences. The initial annotated bibliography contained nearly 300 entries developed collaboratively by Brabenec and Gene Chase.

Over decades, the collection grew to approximately 350 items after computerization and additions. In 2020, the creators decided to develop an expanded second edition. Gene Chase passed away from COVID-19 shortly after this decision. The current work incorporates materials from both prior editions into a Zotero database.

The revised criteria admit entries that deal substantively with how mathematics connects to faith, theology, or worldview. The expanded scope includes the mathematical sciences broadly and multiple religious perspectives, extending beyond traditional published materials to include blogs, interviews, and videos. Since beginning the database in 2023, the compiler has catalogued and annotated more than five times as many items as the earlier editions combined.

### Using the Zotero Library

The Zotero interface has three panels:

- **Left panel** — organizes entries into collections and subcollections (expandable via arrows), with tags and keywords listed below.
- **Middle panel** — displays entries with sortable columns for title, creator, item type, publication details, date, and attachments.
- **Right panel** — shows full metadata for a selected entry, including abstracts, notes, tags, attachments, and related items.

**Abstracts** are marked by contributor initials: [CJ] for the compiler Calvin Jongsma, [GBC] for Gene Chase, [WJB] for Jim Bradley, [HVB] and [DFMS] for earlier contributors, [P] for publishers, and [A] for authors.

**Search** — The search box permits filtering by title, creator, year, and full-text content within the selected collection.

Users can access PDFs directly, retrieve information for obtaining copies, and export entries to personal databases or bibliographies.
